# Review: failures to expect

Check every shot list and every generated clip against this list before calling it done.

From the basic moves:

- **Too many moves in one short shot.** "Push, orbit, climb, then pull out fast" in a few seconds and identity, space and continuity collapse together. Keep one main move; cut for the rest.
- **Subject and camera fighting.** The subject runs at the camera while it pushes in, and the distance collapses. Decide: does the camera meet them, wait, or back away? "Hold two metres, subject size constant" beats "tracking".
- **Direction with no start or end.** "Pan right" may pan until the clip ends. "Start on the empty doorway, pan right to the man at the window, stop at medium" seals it.
- **Mixing a move and a zoom.** "Push in and zoom in and keep the background fixed" is several competing instructions. Unless you want a dolly zoom, choose: push for walking in, zoom for optical enlargement.
- **Every shot moving.** Constant pushing, pulling, panning and sliding remove the viewer's reference point. A static shot holds information and makes the next move hit harder. If the action, background or dialogue is already busy, try a static version first.

From the advanced moves:

- **A device name with no route.** "Drone shot", "gimbal shot" describe gear. Add height, direction, speed, who it follows and where it stops.
- **Handheld as random shake.** Give it a controlled strength: start with "slight footstep bob, restrained breathing drift", add more only for impact.
- **The hero's whole body in POV.** POV sees hands, feet and sleeves, not its own face. Don't ask for "first person" and "hero full front" together unless there's a mirror.
- **FPV with no executable path.** "Fly through the city" is too big. Break it into three or four waypoints and say what to go around and what to go through.
- **Dolly zoom where the subject changes size.** Put "head-and-shoulders size constant, always centred" in the prompt, then the opposite directions for camera and lens. Give the background a corridor, a row of trees or door frames for depth.
- **A long take that sneaks in a cut.** "One-shot" alone can still jump. Add "single continuous shot, no cuts, no teleporting, consistent direction", and make each stage start where the last one ended.

Not every model renders advanced angles, lenses and optical effects reliably, and results depend on the whole prompt and the scene. A vocabulary buys precision, not a guaranteed first-try result. Get one subject, one action and one move working first, then add lens, foreground, complex staging and sound.

## Sequence failures

Check the whole cut, not just each shot:

- **Screen direction flips.** A subject or vehicle travelling toward frame right suddenly travels left between clips. Keep one direction per sequence unless the story turns.
- **No geography.** Action starts before the audience knows where subject, threat and safety are. Add one layout shot first.
- **No scale reference.** A wide shot of something huge with nothing of known size in it reads small. Add a figure, a vehicle, a window.
- **Flat rhythm.** Every shot the same length. Shorten shots toward the climax; hold one beat longer than comfortable before the reveal.
- **No still moment.** Everything moves, so nothing lands. Place one held, near-silent beat before the biggest moment.
- **Shots that change nothing.** A shot that doesn't change what the audience knows or feels is decoration. Cut it.
- **Cuts off the beat** in a music-led piece, or a title that lands between downbeats.
- **A first frame that can't be the cover.** Most platforms show frame 0 as the thumbnail. Opening on black, on an empty frame or on half-typed text wastes it.
